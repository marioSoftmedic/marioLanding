**Law 21.668 requires healthcare providers to adopt the measures needed to make their record systems interoperable.** The statute amends Law 20.584 and governs access to clinical records by professionals directly involved in care, subject to the applicable professional secrecy and confidentiality duties.

The statute was published on May 28, 2024. Its official title is "Establishes the interoperability of clinical records," and the starting point should be the <a href="https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=1203827" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">official Law 21.668 record in LeyChile, maintained by Chile's Library of Congress</a>, not a commercial interpretation.

## What Law 21.668 requires

The statutory text establishes three relevant foundations:

1. Healthcare providers must adopt measures that enable clinical record interoperability.
2. Professionals directly involved in care may access the record under the statutory terms and remain subject to professional secrecy and confidentiality.
3. The Ministry of Health must update the regulation that specifies conditions, procedures, and standards for clinical records and their interoperability.

The law gave the Ministry of Health 18 months from publication to update that regulation. **The statute itself does not name FHIR, SNOMED CT, LOINC, or AIToF, nor does it define a rollout based on provider size.** Those details should not be presented as legal mandates without an official technical rule that establishes them.

## Where FHIR fits

<a href="https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/overview.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">FHIR R4</a> is an HL7 standard for exchanging healthcare information electronically through structured resources. It can represent patients, requests, and results, but FHIR does not become mandatory in Chile merely because it is technically suitable.

The <a href="https://hl7chile.cl/fhir/ig/clcore/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">HL7 Chile Core-CL Implementation Guide</a> provides profiles and terminology adapted to the national context. Core-CL is a concrete technical reference for implementers; a claim of legal obligation still requires checking the regulation and rules applicable to the specific case.

## What this means for a clinical laboratory

An interoperable workflow can connect an order to its result using resources such as `ServiceRequest`, `Observation`, and `DiagnosticReport`. That architecture avoids relying only on PDFs, but it does not replace identity, authorization, consent, traceability, or governance controls.

The implementation path is covered in [FHIR and Law 21.668: the adapter layer](/en/blog/2026-04-18-fhir-ley-21-668-interoperabilidad-chile) and [how DiagnosticReport returns a result to the physician](/en/blog/2026-04-22-fhir-diagnostic-report-resultado-laboratorio-medico).

## Interoperability does not mean open access

FHIR defines exchange models, not a complete security system. The <a href="https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/security.html" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">official FHIR R4 security guidance</a> says a clinical implementation must address authentication, authorization, transport encryption, audit, and access control. Sharing data does not authorize unrestricted exposure.

## AI transparency: an experimental track, not a mandate

<a href="https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/aitransparency-ig/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">AI Transparency on FHIR</a> is an HL7 continuous-build draft exploring how to record AI involvement through labels and provenance resources. Its own site warns that it changes regularly and is not an authorized publication. It should not be presented as a current Chilean requirement or an inevitable future mandate.

## What changes for Examya

Law 21.668 is a design and governance constraint, not automatic product certification. The [Examya editorial entity page](/en/examya) gathers the case, architecture, and related articles; [examya.cl](https://examya.cl/) retains transactional intent. For interoperability-specific commercial work, the separate reference is [Fhirex](https://fhirex.examya.cl/).

The useful conclusion is bounded: **interoperability requires combining the legal mandate with verifiable technical profiles and security controls**. Choosing FHIR can be a sound implementation decision, but it remains an implementation choice until an applicable rule says something more specific.
