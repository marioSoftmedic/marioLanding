## 1. The Hook
It is Saturday morning. You need to quote some medical exams ordered by your specialist. You open WhatsApp, take a quick photo of the crumpled paper on the table, send it to the Examya channel, and the system replies asking for a new image. You wonder why artificial intelligence stumbles over something that seems as simple as reading a photo.

## 2. What No One Tells You
Behind that automated WhatsApp reply is an artificial intelligence engine (an OCR) and a team of human validators working together. In healthcare, clinical precision is always above blind speed. If the system does not have a high level of confidence in reading an exam, the correct guardrail is to stop and ask for a new image, rather than generating an incorrect quote or confusing one test with another.

## 3. The Real Data
Upon reviewing Examya's support logs, we confirmed that 40% of image re-requests do not happen due to system failures or poor camera quality, but rather three simple capture issues:
1. **Harsh shadows:** The phone blocks the light source, darkening the text.
2. **Cropped documents:** Close-up photos that leave out the RUT (ID) or signature.
3. **Creases and wrinkles:** Folded papers that distort the words.

## 4. How We Designed the Validator
To resolve this without wasting the patient's time, we implemented a validator in the Examya admission flow. Before processing the image with the heavy OCR engine, the system performs a quick analysis to verify that the document is complete (identifying the presence of the RUT at the top and the doctor's signature/stamp at the bottom) and that the contrast is sufficient for a safe reading.

## 5. The Shadow Gotcha
The most interesting technical challenge occurred with the phone's own shadow. If a shadow falls directly over an exam code, the OCR returns broken characters. Instead of trying to correct the text using language models (which could cause dangerous hallucinations in health data), the validator calculates the light distribution in the image. If it detects a critical dark area, it immediately prompts the patient to tilt their phone.

## 6. Zoom Out
Helping the system read your prescription reduces administrative reprocessing. A clear photo translates into fewer back-and-forth messages, shorter wait times, and an accurate quote ready for laboratory approval. It is a workflow where patient care begins right from the phone's camera.

## 7. What's Next
We are working to incorporate an interactive guide within the WhatsApp chat. If the validator detects a lighting or framing issue, it will visually show you in real-time how to adjust the phone so your second attempt is instantly successful.
