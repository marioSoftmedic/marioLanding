It is 8:30 in the morning. You open the government procurement portal and face an endless stream of newly published tenders: hundreds of opportunity notices, generic descriptions, and dozens of compressed `.zip` or `.rar` attachments containing administrative bidding terms.

For any B2B company or technology provider bidding on public contracts, this daily routine is an exhausting operational drain. A commercial and engineering team can easily spend four or five hours parsing administrative annexes only to discover, on page 47 of the special clauses, an insurmountable blocker: the tender requires five consecutive years of audited billing in an ultra-specific category or a bank guarantee bond that disproportionately drains working capital.

Manual prospecting in public procurement suffers from a structural flaw: teams are not failing due to a lack of available tenders; they are suffering from operational blindness caused by the sheer volume of unstructured documents and the compounding cost of late disqualification.

## The Cost of Late Disqualification in Public Bidding

In standard B2B private sales, qualifying a prospect often takes a quick discovery call covering budget, authority, and timelines. In the public sector, the dynamics are inverted: all information is open and transparent, yet intentionally scattered across dozens of legal documents written in dense administrative jargon.

The true opportunity cost for an engineering and commercial team is not drafting the technical bid; it is the hours spent analyzing tenders they never had a realistic path to bid on. If your team takes two hours to discover that a public tender mandates an obscure certification you do not hold, those two hours were stolen from crafting a winning technical proposal where you held a distinct competitive advantage.

Passive manual monitoring—searching portals with broad keywords and downloading PDFs indiscriminately—leads to two damaging outcomes:

1. **Decision fatigue:** high-margin strategic tenders get overlooked because they were buried under inaccurate procurement categories or obscure procurement codes.
2. **Non-viable submissions:** teams invest significant engineering resources into complex proposals without catching strict administrative barriers, resulting in early disqualification during document opening.

Solving this bottleneck does not require building an AI tool that attempts to write public proposals automatically. The primary engineering goal is much simpler and more impactful: build a **fast disqualification mechanism** that extracts disqualifying criteria and evaluates technical viability within the first five minutes.

## Deconstructing Public Tenders: What to Catch Before Opening an Editor

When analyzing procurement documents from platforms like Chile's Mercado Público, over 80% of disqualifications or commercial mismatches stem from four predictable variables:

- **Strict admissibility requirements:** specific ISO certifications, regulatory permits, security clearances, or non-negotiable minimum company track records.
- **Financial liquidity and guarantee instruments:** mandatory bid bonds, performance bonds, insurance policies with custom clauses, and rapid delivery schedules that tie up cash flow.
- **Clarification inquiry windows:** tight deadlines for the official question-and-answer period (often only 48 to 72 hours from publication), where technical ambiguities must be resolved.
- **Evaluation and weighting scorecards:** scoring matrices where price may represent 70% against 30% technical weight, or vice versa, dictating whether the potential profit margin justifies the operational effort.

When an automated pipeline extracts these four variables deterministically into structured records, the commercial workflow shifts from passive document skimming to an actionable decision board.

## Building the Scouting Architecture: Next.js, TypeScript, and Agentic Extraction

To explore this workflow, we built **RadarPyme**, an exploratory functional proof of concept and technical portfolio application designed to decouple public tender ingestion and streamline commercial qualification.

The system was engineered on an end-to-end typed stack: Next.js with TypeScript, automated ingestion workers, and specialized extraction agents constrained by strict Zod schema validation.

```text
SCOUTING PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE:

  [ Public Procurement API / Portal ]
                  │
                  ▼
  [ Ingestion & Normalization ]   ───► Base metadata extraction
                  │
                  ▼
  [ Deterministic Rule Filter ]   ───► Business constraints (budget, region, industry)
                  │ (Passes filter)
                  ▼
  [ Extraction Agent ]            ───► Document parsing with typed Zod schemas
                  │
                  ▼
  [ Decision Pipeline ]           ───► Discarded | Under Review | Qualified Candidates
```

The core design principle here is to never delegate business logic or initial filtering to the language model. Feeding 90-page administrative tender documents directly to an LLM without preprocessing is expensive, slow, and prone to hallucinating numerical figures.

Instead, the system applies deterministic TypeScript filters first (budget ranges, geographical regions, submission deadlines). Only when a tender clears these minimum operational thresholds does the document payload enter the structured extraction agent.

### Typed Extraction with Zod Schemas

To ensure that the agent's output can be safely consumed by the pipeline without runtime crashes, we enforce a strict contract using TypeScript schemas:

```typescript
import { z } from "zod";

export const TenderQualificationSchema = z.object({
  tenderId: z.string().describe("Official public procurement tender ID"),
  title: z.string().describe("Tender title or procurement call"),
  purchasingEntity: z.string().describe("Public agency or ministry requesting the bid"),
  estimatedBudgetClp: z.number().nullable().describe("Estimated budget in local currency (CLP)"),
  submissionDeadline: z.string().describe("Bid submission closing date and time"),
  queryDeadline: z.string().describe("Deadline to submit clarification questions"),
  warrantyBondRequired: z.boolean().describe("Whether a bid bond or bank guarantee is required"),
  warrantyAmountClp: z.number().nullable().describe("Required guarantee bond amount"),
  mandatoryCertifications: z.array(z.string()).describe("Mandatory exclusions or technical certifications"),
  minimumExperienceYears: z.number().default(0).describe("Minimum required years of verified corporate experience"),
  disqualifyingClauses: z.array(z.string()).describe("Disqualifying administrative or technical clauses"),
  evaluationCriteria: z.array(
    z.object({
      criterion: z.string(),
      weightPercentage: z.number(),
    })
  ).describe("Scoring matrix for technical and commercial evaluation"),
});

export type TenderQualification = z.infer<typeof TenderQualificationSchema>;
```

By constraining the model to output valid JSON matching this schema, we eliminate conversational filler and produce structured records that immediately populate the commercial pipeline board.

## Engineering Trade-offs: Pre-Filtering Saves Tokens and Reduces False Positives

During early prototyping, the most tempting mistake was sending entire document attachments into multimodal vision models or optical character recognition engines.

Public procurement attachments present nasty real-world edge cases: low-resolution scanned legal deeds, corrupted tables, and dozens of pages of generic municipal decrees that carry zero commercial relevance.

The key breakthrough came not from tweaking model temperatures or swapping foundation models, but from introducing a deterministic **heuristic pre-filtering phase**:

1. **Financial threshold checks:** if the required bid bond amount exceeds the company's designated liquidity parameters, the tender is automatically tagged as discarded with the exact reason logged, avoiding unnecessary inference cost.
2. **Relevant text slicing:** instead of passing all 80 pages of a specification, we slice sections containing strict admissibility keywords ("minimum criteria", "grounds for rejection", "technical evaluation").
3. **Timeline cross-checks:** if the official clarification window has already elapsed and the specifications contain ambiguous technical criteria, the system flags the operational risk before engineers commit time to drafting.

Pairing deterministic rules with targeted LLM extraction ensures that computational spend is concentrated exclusively on qualified opportunities.

## Moving from Passive Monitoring to an Actionable Pipeline

Modernizing how B2B teams engage with government procurement requires shifting the underlying mental model. Traditional monitoring is passive and reactive: an analyst sifts through inbox notifications hoping to spot relevant keywords.

An agent-driven pipeline converts that monitoring into a structured workflow with explicit statuses:

- **Discarded (with explicit reason):** tenders that fail core criteria (experience, certifications, or margin thresholds). The team spends zero minutes debating them.
- **Under Technical Review:** viable tenders with specific ambiguities that require submitting clarification questions to the official public forum before the deadline.
- **Qualified Candidates:** opportunities with verified admissibility, balanced evaluation criteria, and delivery timelines compatible with current team capacity.

The goal is not to promise guaranteed contract awards—final award decisions ultimately depend on proposal quality, pricing, and government evaluation panels. The true engineering leverage lies in **eliminating operational blindness**: giving commercial and technical teams the visibility to focus their energy entirely where they have a legitimate, defensible path to compete.
