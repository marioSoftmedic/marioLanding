In digital health architectures processing outpatient prescriptions and lab orders, roughly 80% of automated retrials are not caused by flawed machine learning models or illegible doctor handwriting. They happen because of four basic optical issues when snapping the photo on a smartphone.

## The optical physics behind computer vision

When a physical piece of paper moves from a table to a messaging conversation, optical factors come into play that human eyes compensate for naturally, but that severely degrade computer vision pipelines:

1. **Cast shadows:** Tilting the phone directly above the paper causes the device or your hand to block the light source, creating an uneven dark gradient across the text.
2. **Specular flash glare:** Firing the flash directly at semi-gloss paper or thermal receipts creates white hot spots of overexposure that completely wash out the ink.
3. **Missing metadata from tight cropping:** Cropping in too closely cuts off the document corners where vital administrative data resides (National ID/RUT, date, physician signature, and institutional stamp).
4. **Motion blur:** Taking a handheld shot in mid-air introduces micro-vibrations that smudge neighboring characters together.

The good news is that preventing these four pitfalls takes exactly ten seconds before pressing "Send".

  hasSufficientContrast: boolean;
  specularGlareDetected: boolean;

export function assessMedicalOrderCapture(

## 4 golden rules for a flawless capture on the first try

To ensure your medical order processes instantly without retrials, follow these four practical rules:

### 1. Flat overhead or natural light (no phone shadows)
Place the medical order flat on a table or desk. Ensure light comes from an overhead fixture or diffuse side lighting (such as a nearby window). Hold the phone about 30 centimeters away and slightly back from your torso to prevent your phone's shadow from falling over the written test names.

### 2. Keep the camera flash off
Always disable the flash. A direct camera flash creates a concentrated specular glare in the center of the sheet, blinding optical sensors and destroying the contrast of faint ink or light stamps. Uniform ambient lighting consistently outperforms direct flash.

### 3. Perpendicular framing with all 4 corners visible
Hold your phone parallel to the table (a 90-degree angle relative to the document) and capture all four corners of the page. In Chile, healthcare regulations (Law 20,584 and national prescription standards) require validating critical administrative fields before calculating co-payments:
- Patient full name and National ID (RUT).
- Issuing date of the medical order.
- Clear itemization of requested laboratory tests.
- Physician signature and professional registration stamp.

Cropping out the margins to zoom in prevents the system from verifying the formal validity of the document.

### 4. A 2-second zoom check before sending
Before hitting send in WhatsApp, tap the photo preview and quickly zoom in on the first test name. If you can read the handwriting and numbers effortlessly, the OCR engine and the laboratory clinical team will extract them with high confidence. If it looks blurry to you, retake the photo with your hands resting steadily on the table edge.
