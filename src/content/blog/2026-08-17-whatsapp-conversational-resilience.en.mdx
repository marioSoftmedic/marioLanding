## Patients never follow the script

In architecture diagrams, a WhatsApp medical quotation funnel looks like a clean pipeline: the patient greets, uploads their prescription, the engine quotes, and the patient confirms and pays.

In production with real patients, that happy path accounts for less than 40% of interactions.

A patient receives a quote with a lab code and asks: *“What is the 1803016 blood test for?”* or *“Do I need to fast before this test?”*. Another uploads a blurry photo taken under dim lighting in a clinic waiting room.

If your state machine is rigid, an educational question resets the entire conversation back to the main menu (wiping the cart), while a blurry photo triggers an infinite loop of *“Please try uploading again”*. In both cases, the result is the same: immediate drop-off and user frustration.

In healthcare, technical rigidity kills patient trust.

## The transactional state dilemma

When building a transactional conversational assistant (like Shuri in Examya), two opposing requirements collide:

1. **Transactional determinism:** You must guarantee that selected exams, pricing agreements, and cart items remain immutable during checkout.
2. **Conversational flexibility:** The patient needs to pause, ask clarifying questions about their lab tests, and resume without friction.

Treating every message with a stateless LLM loses transactional control. Using a rigid decision tree breaks down the moment a user steps off-script.

## Our solution: Educational sub-intents and contextual branching

To solve the primary mid-funnel drop-off, we refactored our intent router (`intent-router.ts`) to treat `exam_inquiry` as a non-destructive sub-task.

```typescript
// Simplified intent router snippet
export async function routeShuriIntent(
  incomingMessage: string,
  sessionContext: ConversationContext
): Promise<RoutingDecision> {
  const currentStage = sessionContext.stage;

  // If in an active quote or confirmation stage
  if (currentStage === ConversationStage.QUOTING || currentStage === ConversationStage.AWAITING_CONFIRMATION) {
    const isEducationalQuery = await detectExamInquiryIntent(incomingMessage);
    
    if (isEducationalQuery) {
      return {
        action: 'HANDLE_EDUCATIONAL_INQUIRY',
        preserveState: true,
        resumeStage: currentStage,
      };
    }
  }

  return evaluateStandardTransitions(incomingMessage, sessionContext);
}
```

When a patient asks about a test during the quote flow:
- The router detects the informational catalog query.
- Shuri explains in clear, compassionate language what the test evaluates and basic fasting requirements.
- **State is preserved:** The cart, medical codes, and pricing breakdown remain intact.
- Shuri seamlessly prompts the next step: *“Would you like to continue confirming your order?”*.

## Circuit breakers and graceful OCR degradation

The second drop-off point happens when OCR cannot parse a handwritten or low-contrast medical prescription.

Asking the patient to *“upload the photo again”* indefinitely guarantees they will abandon the chat.

We implemented a strict retry circuit breaker (`ocr_retry_count >= 2`):

1. **Attempt 1 (Parse Failure):** Shuri asks for a new photo with actionable tips (good lighting, flat placement, no glare).
2. **Attempt 2 (Consecutive Failure):** The circuit breaker trips. OCR processing halts to prevent fatigue, unlocking two rescue branches:
   - Guided manual text entry via assisted catalog search.
   - Immediate human advisor handoff, sending a real-time alert to our operations Slack channel with the full session payload and raw image.

```
[Patient uploads blurry photo 2nd time]
                  │
                  ▼
      [OCR Retry Threshold >= 2]
                  │
          ┌───────┴───────┐
          ▼               ▼
   [Manual Entry]   [Slack Handoff Alert]
  (Assisted Search) (Advisor takes over)
```

## Production data and learnings

After deploying these fixes in production:
- We eliminated 100% of infinite OCR retry loops.
- Patients asking educational questions successfully completed their medical orders without session restarts.
- Handoff response times dropped significantly because advisors receive pre-packaged context directly in Slack.

## Zoom out: what this means for HealthTech

In digital healthcare, AI does not replace human empathy or clinical staff; it coordinates them.

A resilient system isn't one that pretends to have 100% mathematical accuracy on every raw photo or sentence, but one that knows **when to educate**, **when to degrade gracefully**, and **when to hand the wheel to a human**.
